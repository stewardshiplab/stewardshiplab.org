[ecosyste.ms](https://ecosyste.ms) is the world's largest public-access source of
data on Open Source software. It holds data on 16.6 million packages, 357 million repositories, and 25.8
billion dependency edges, enabling it to know which packages are most critical to society, who maintains them,
and how well they're able to handle the pressures placed upon them. Its users have included Microsoft, the
Linux Foundation, the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, and the Sovereign Tech Agency.

Despite this, ecosyste.ms is currently supported by very little funding from a single-digit number of
commercial clients.

Additional funding would help ensure the future sustainability of the service. And it would allow us to
develop features to enable policymakers and security researchers worldwide to do their work.

<h3>Goals</h3>
<div class="program__features">
  <div class="program__feature">
    <h4>Providing data on CVE reachability</h4>
    <div>
      Package registry data doesn't provide the full picture on CVE reachability. We are able to analyse
      vendored binaries, patches, and repackaged dependencies to provide richer data on CVE reachability than is
      currently available.
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="program__feature">
    <h4>Helping funders reach maintainers</h4>
    <div>
      <a href="https://funds.ecosyste.ms/" target="_blank">ecosyste.ms Funds</a> already allows companies to
      fund OSS they depend on. We aim to expand this service to donors to give to custom funds,
      increasing the number of maintainers receiving financial support.
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="program__feature">
    <h4>Making adoption easier</h4>
    <div>
      Adopting ecosyste.ms means that researchers do not need to duplicate the work of collecting data on
      millions of packages. The easier we make adoption, the more time and effort we save them.
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="program__feature">
    <h4>Identifying OSS used in scientific research</h4>
    <div>
      It is difficult to trace the dependency relationship between a scientific paper and the software used in
      its research. We plan to augment our data to identify the packages most critical to scientific research.
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
